6.2
Grid Convergence Study
Regretably, a three-mesh refinement study for the full-loop study could not be completed
across the entire simulation matrix. The full-loop simulation pipeline leveraged the faster
convergence of the coarse mesh to converge to a solution from the initial conditions before
mapping the converged solution from the coarse mesh to the next finer mesh. After mapping
the solution to the medium refinement mesh, the simulation experiences start-up instability
before settling to the new converged state within the refined mesh. The decision ultimately
boiled down to presenting a mix of settled and mostly settled fine-mesh cases with the
full exclusion of the fine-mesh turbulence cases, or the two-refinement study that could
present confident and honest results across the full matrix. The latter option was chosen.
The cost of this includes Richardson extrapolation of the QoIs, discussion of monotonic vs
potential oscillatory convergence, and a larger unum, for each case, but earns a discussion
of the performance of the turbulent models. This is particularly interesting following the
discussion in Section 5.2.3 where we predict to see the turbulent model out-perform the
laminar counterpart on account of the long hydrodynamic entry lengths for water cases 3
and 4.
Thankfully, Roache provides procedure for cases such as this; where, for performing GCI
on two mesh refinements, the value of Fs used in Equation 3.7 takes the value of 3 instead
of 1.25 [37].
