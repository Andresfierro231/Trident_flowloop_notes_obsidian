Abstract
Natural circulation loops are integral to passive safety systems in advanced nuclear reactor
designs, yet their computational modeling remains challenging due to the strong coupling
between buoyancy-driven flow, conjugate heat transfer, and temperature-dependent fluid
properties. This thesis works to demonstrate a comprehensive Verification and Validation
study of a three-dimensional compuational fluid dynamics simulation of the single-phase nat-
ural convection experimental facility operated by Texas A&M University and validate this
model against the experimental data of Reis, Seo, and Hassan (2024). The facility used two
fluids, water and HITEC molten salt, a eutectic nitrate salt mix chosen for its low melting
point and similar behavior to molten salts intended for use in molten salt reactors. For both
working fluids, four experiments were conducted each, varying the loop’s primary heater
power between each case and studying the effects that change had on the velocity profiles
within the system. Simulations of the system were performed in OpenFOAM v13 using
a structured, fully-conformal, purely-hexahedral mesh generated with the classy_blocks
library python library. A segmented first-order model was first built to reduce energy ac-
counting uncertainties associated with the experiment, and better account for passive heat
losses throughout the system.
