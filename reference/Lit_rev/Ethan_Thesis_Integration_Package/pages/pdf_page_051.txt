4.2.3
Cooling System Measurements
The cooling system seen on the top leg of the loop is a concentric counter-flow heat exchanger,
using room temperature air as the coolant for the experimental facility. The heat exchanger
ran at a constant volumetric flow rate of 188 L/min for the water cases and 37 L/min for
the salt cases. The heat exchanger jacket was 1 foot long and used two reducer fittings
(McMaster: 44705K661) on either side of the jacket to mount the cooling jacket onto the
loop.
To determine the heat removed by the heat exchanger, temperature measurements were
taken by the same K-type thermocouples at the coolant’s inlet and outlet and then Reis et
al. determined the energy loss from the heat exchanger by evaluating the properties of air
at the average temperature of the inlet and outlet and using the equation
Q = ˙mCp(Tout −Tin).
