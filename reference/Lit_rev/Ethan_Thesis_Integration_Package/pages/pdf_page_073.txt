5.2.3
Turbulence Modeling
Initially, all the simulations in this work were scoped to only examine laminar modeling of
the experiments as the pipe Reynolds numbers (ReD = 75-1110) are well below the critical
value for turbulent transition (ReD ≈2300) in typical pipe flow. However, early simulation
attempts did not recreate the skewed velocity profile shape seen in water tests 3 and 4 and the
flucuations present in the water tests were much greater than the salt test simulations. The
idea that the salt tests were "more laminar" than the water tests led to the idea that some
amount of turbulence was being incurred despite being below the typical critical Reynolds
number. As mentioned in Section 2.1 the long entry lengths delay or prevent full-velocity
development, which would give creedance to shifting the characteristic length of the flow
from the typical dimension of the pipe’s inner diameter, to the height of the loop. Calcu-
lating the Rayleigh number of the system using this characteristic length produces values
exceeding 1010, which is known as the critical threshold for turbulence in cases involving
natural circulation. Typically in a pipe flow such as this, the transition to turbulence pre-
dicted by a critical Rayleigh number would be suppressed by the full development of the
laminar velocity profile before the transition could occur, but because of our provably long
hydrodynamic entry lengths, we can predict to see some level of turbulence, especially in
water cases 3 and 4; which are exactly the two cases that whose velocity profiles are better
captured by turbulence modeling than laminar modeling as seen in Chapter 6.
As any turbulence present was theorized to be in the midst of transitioning from laminar
flow, the transitional turbulence model, k −ω SSTLM was chosen as the primary modeling
choice. Secondarily, when researching the k−ω SSTLM model that OpenFOAM uses, it is the
standard formulation that assumes the production of turbulent kinetic energy from buoyant
effects is negligible. So, a codedFvModel was made to reintroduce the buoyant production of
turbulent kinetic energy when the extension "*_Gb" was found on a simulation case name.
