For these calculations, the velocity data captured in the full-field PIV data was assumed
to be symmetrical for each case, then the right half of the data was used for U(r), where
r = 0 is the centerline and R = Dtest/2 is the right wall of the test section. Dtest is the
inner diameter of the test section (0.83") and Dpipe is the inner diameter of the stainless steel
pipe used in the majority of the loop (0.93"). No post-processing scripts could be found by
the experimentalists to confirm this exact implementation, but it reproduces the tabulated
values for U∞within 1.3 % for the available data.
The "available data" is specified apart from all test cases because the full-field velocity
data for each water case had been lost, deleted, or for some other reason was unrecoverable
despite extensive efforts by the experimentalists to locate and provide that data. Thankfully,
the full-field velocity data for the salt cases was recovered and subsequently provided, so we
are able to test reconstruction methodologies for QoIs that require the full-field velocity data
to bound the uncertainty attached to any given reconstruction. For instance, we are able
to attempt an extraction of the full-field velocity data for each of the water cases based on
Figure 14 from Reis et al. [9].
