The four legs are:
• Lower leg (heater): this leg is wrapped with 120V AC, resistance heaters (McMaster:
4550T123) capable of outputting 312 W each that served as the primary heat input
for the system. Three of these heaters were connected in series to wrap the whole of
the bottom leg with a pitch between wraps of 1/2" to 1" apart.
• Left leg (riser): most notably, this leg contains the primary focus of the experimental
configuration, a quartz glass test section measuring 186 mm of exposed length. The
test section had a nominal ID of 21 mm and OD of 1". The quartz test section was
fitted into the middle of the left leg after the 3’ pipe was cut into two segments that
would allow the test section to be installed such that it was centered along the leg’s
length and that the total length of the leg was equal in length to the other three legs of
the loop. The quartz test section’s transparency is what allows PIV measurements to
be taken for the experiment. It is worth noting that the ID of 21 mm is approximately
0.83 inches, which represents a step reduction in ID of 0.10 inches and an approximate
21% reduction in cross sectional area.
• Upper leg (cooler): contains a 1 foot long cooling jacket that serves as a concentric,
counter-flow air-cooled heat exchanger with reducer fittings on either side to mount
the cooling jacket in the center of the upper leg. This heat exchanger is intended as
the primary vector of heat removal from the system as the counterpart of the lower
leg’s heating tape in order to create the temperature difference that drives the system’s
natural circulation. The upper leg is nominally bent in the same way at the lower leg,
but it should be noted that the fittings the cooling jacket is mounted to are essentially
through-wall pipe fittings, resulting in the upper leg being segmented and connected
by the in-line pipe fittings that double as the heat exchanger’s mounting fixture.
