The L2 error norm gives a greater weight to larger errors compared to something like a
simple average (also known as the L1 norm) and the L∞norm only reports the maximum
error. Applying these norms to the thermocouple data is fairly simple since they map 1-
to-1 to the experimental system. However, it is important to note that the location of the
thermocouples in space has its own uncertainty, especially for the wall thermocouples that
lie outside the fluid domain, which will be discussed later in Chapter 6 along side spatial
sensitivity of the centerline probes in high-temperature gradient regions of the facility. For
the velocity profiles, a bit more care is needed since the experimental data is taken at discrete
points in space and the simulation is essentially continuous. The key to accurately applying
the error metrics for a case like this is to sample the simulated data at the equivalent
points in space for the same number of entries so that the calculations can be performed
appropriately.
