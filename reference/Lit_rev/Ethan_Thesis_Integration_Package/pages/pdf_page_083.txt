For the emissivities, engineering values were picked from Table 10.1 in Lienhard and
Lienhard [20] as a baseline for aluminum, quartz glass, and insulation emissivities. The entry
for quartz glass is very near to the experimental cases, so has been neglected for uncertainty
analysis. Similar uncertainty was not provided for the heat exchanger’s flowrate, so has been
excluded from the uncertainty analysis here.
With each input expressed in terms of the QoI, we can summarize their contributions in
Table 5.2. The entries ugeo, uins, umat and uenv are the geometric, insulation, material, and
environmental contributions above, with the propagated QoI given in the subscript. The
geometric uncertainty is calculated from the uncertainties in both test-section and loop-pipe
diameters as well as the loop length. To propagate ugeo to the QoIs of velocity and mass
flow rate, Poiseuille pipe flow scaling laws were assumed applicable; an assumption that is
undermined by the under-developed flow discussed in Section 2.1, but is retained here as an
attempt to bound input uncertainty.
The insulation uncertainty is determined from the first order model being tested with
±0.05 inch insulation perturbations from the modeled thickness. By maintaining the same
boundary conditions, the different insulation configurations produce a different energy flow
necessary to uphold those conditions condtions, allowing us to quantify the change of energy
flow as a function of insulation thickness. From this, we can multiply by the value of ut
to recover an energy flow uncertainty. From here, we can then convert that result to the
uncertainty introduced to the QoI. For temperature, we return the model to the nominal
configuration and uniformly perturb the system’s input temperatures by ±1 K, which can
tell us how the energy flow changes in response to temperature. Dividing our previous energy
flow uncertainty by this result gives us the propagated uncertainty for temperature for this
input. The mass flow rate uncertainty similarly comes from the energy flow uncertainty but
needs to account for the feedback of the fluid viscosity’s response to the predicted change
in temperature for such a predicted change in energy flow in addition to the typical scaling
