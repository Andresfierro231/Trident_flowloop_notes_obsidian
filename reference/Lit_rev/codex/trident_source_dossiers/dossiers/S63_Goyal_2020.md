## Problem, prior work and results

The paper asks how a linearly stable rectangular loop can respond to finite initial disturbances. Its model follows earlier Vijayan/Austregesilo work, considers a horizontal heater/horizontal cooler (HHHC) loop, and uses Genoa Loop#1 geometry from Mousavian et al. Prior stability comparisons also use a BARC case. These are different cases, not one newly measured facility (§§2–3.1,PDF2–5).

Numerical trajectories suggest subcritical behavior: small perturbations decay while larger ones can approach large oscillations at the same nominal conditions (§3.2,Figs6–7,PDF5–8). Other simulations show reversal-like chaotic switching and transient reversals before return to a steady state (§§3.3–3.4,Figs8–10,PDF6–10). The value is a direct warning that local linear stability is insufficient to certify finite-disturbance behavior.

Claims in the introduction that future MSRs operate entirely by thermosiphoning are broad motivational statements. This source does not substantiate that claim for all MSR concepts; do not repeat it as a reactor-design fact.

## Geometry, physics and source laws

Figure1,PDF3 represents a constant-diameter rectangular loop with externally coiled Nichrome heating and a coaxial cooler; secondary inlet temperature and flow are maintained. The reduced equations replace the secondary system with an overall cooler coefficient U and prescribed Tsec. Assumptions: incompressible Boussinesq, 1D, insulated nonheated walls, negligible axial fluid conduction, wall thermal conductivity/storage effects absent from the reported state equations, negligible viscous heating (§2.1,PDF3).

W is mass flow in kg/s, not volumetric flow or mass flux. Reported energy equation:
\[
T_t+uT_s=C(s,T),\quad
C_h=\frac{4q''}{\rho c_pD},\quad
C_c=\frac{4U(T_{\rm sec}-T)}{\rho c_pD}.
\]
These are restatements of Eq.(3) and its definitions, PDF3. Both sources have K/s units. At fixed secondary temperature the cooler is a relaxation boundary law; it is not prescribed extracted power.

The Darcy drag law is f=p/Re^b, with laminar(64,1), transition(1.2036,.416) and turbulent(.316,.25) coefficients (§2.1,Eq6,PDF3). Here p is a friction correlation coefficient, also used elsewhere as pressure/parameter: retain context. The paper uses distinct regime laws rather than establishing a universal smooth transition model.

Temperature is nondimensionalized by the steady heater temperature rise; flow by steady Wss; time by loop inventory turnover time. Grm and Stm characterize the maps. Eqs.(9)–(11),PDF4 are spatial/steady-looking energy statements; the actual transient semidiscretization in Eqs.(14)–(17) includes time derivatives. They must not be interchanged in implementation.

## Numerical procedure and verification

Spatial upwind-like temperature differences yield many ODEs, with an equilibrium Jacobian and dominant eigenvalues used for local stability (§2.3,PDF4–5). The printed Jacobian Eq.(18) was visually checked and is a conventional derivative matrix; poor OCR should not be mistaken for a mathematical error.

The authors compare1090 nodes at5mm with2180 nodes at2.5mm and report little boundary change;5mm is used thereafter (§3.1,Fig4,PDF5–6). Explicit temperature stepping uses nondimensional Δτ=.0001; momentum is implicit with Newton iteration (§3.2,PDF5). This is evidence of one spatial sensitivity check, not complete space/time convergence of all nonlinear statistics.

Transient experiments in the model start at steady thermal conditions and perturb mass flow. They therefore probe selected directions in state space, not every physically possible heater, cooler, wall or thermal-profile perturbation. Fig7 demonstrates different outcomes; it does not establish the whole basin boundary by itself.

AppendixA,PDF10–11 estimates box-counting dimensions of projected trajectories using five reported box sizes(.6,.36,.216,.1296,.07776). It reports1.7707 and1.8008. Those estimates and sensitive trajectories support complex behavior but do not alone prove a chaotic invariant attractor: finite sampling, projection, scale range and numerical error matter. No Lyapunov-spectrum or rigorous attractor proof is supplied here.

## Implementation ambiguities and skeptical appraisal

Reported drag expressions contain W² or \(\omega^{2-b}\), while reversal trajectories include negative flow. Noninteger powers of negative flow are not a real-valued signed drag convention. The printed upstream differences also assume one ordering. A sign-aware drag and reversed-flow flux rule may have been implemented, but are not established by these equations. Do not infer defective executed code; require source/code clarification before copying.

The paper infers unstable cycles/subcritical Hopf structure from transients; it does not report full unstable-cycle continuation and a first Lyapunov coefficient. Some stability labels and initial-perturbation exponents vary between prose/captions/appendix. Treat topology and exact thresholds as needing reproduction, while retaining the robust lesson that finite disturbances can matter.

No new measurement campaign, uncertainty distribution, blind calibration/validation split or physical perturbation probability is provided. A large numerical perturbation is not a measured disturbance envelope for TRIDENT.

## TRIDENT implications and coverage

For transitions, define a finite-time outcome and disturbance envelope before a stability claim. A margin to the local Jacobian boundary does not automatically guarantee recovery, lack of reversal or thermal limits. Sparse thermal observations may not distinguish those outcomes; use this as a conditional extension rather than a required thesis centerpiece.
