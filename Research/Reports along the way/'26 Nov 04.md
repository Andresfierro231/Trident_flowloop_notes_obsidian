| Symbol     | Meaning                                                      | Units |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----- |
| T          | Temperature represented by the model                         | K     |
| T_a        | Surrounding air temperature                                  | K     |
| C          | Heat needed to warm the participating assembly by one kelvin | J/K   |
| G          | Heat lost per kelvin of assembly-to-air difference           | W/K   |
| Pnom​      | Nominal heater generation from its accepted setting model    | W     |
| η          | Fraction assigned to this modeled assembly                   | 1     |
| P=η\*Pnom​ | Power actually assigned to this model state                  | W     |
The balance says **change in stored heat = input − heat loss**:
$$

C\frac{dT}{dt}=P-G(T-T_a).

$$
When ambient temperature is constant, define the temperature rise θ=T−Ta​. With the heater off, 
$$\theta(t)=\theta(0)\exp(-t/\tau),\qquad \tau=C/G.$$
Doubling both C and G leaves that cooling history unchanged. Cooling tells us **how fast**, but does not separately determine **how much heat is stored** and **how easily heat escapes**. If delivered input and steady temperature are known, $G=P/θ_{steady}$ fixes the loss scale and 
$C=τ*G$ then fixes total storage.
