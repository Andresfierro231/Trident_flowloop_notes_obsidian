As previously mentioned, the loop’s four legs are connected to each other by four fit-
tings. Three of the four junctions are cross-fittings that connect the legs of the loop. These
cross-fittings are either capped off on their unused ports or have pipe extensions that serve
some other experimental purpose; the top right junction connects to the melting pot for
inputting the working fluid, the lower right junction’s lower port connects to the drain tank
for emptying the loop, and the lower left junction’s bottom port was fitted with a bubble
injection valve for experiments outside the scope of this thesis. The remaining junction in
in the upper left corner was a tee-junction whose upper port served as an off-gas vent for
the loop for the aforementioned bubble injection experiments. Each of these junctions have
an expanded outer diameter to the accommodate the standard pipe, with an inner diameter
of 25.4 mm (1 in) for their ports and an for the interior region of the junction, each fitting
had an inner diameter of 22.225 mm (7/8 in) for each of the intersecting cylindrical regions
of the junction.
4.1.2
Thermal Insulation
For each experiment, the loop is insulated with ceramic fiber insulation sourced from McMaster-
Carr (code 93315K18) to reduce passive heat losses to the environment. This insulation was
configured differently between the salt and water experiments due to the different operating
temperatures, with extra insulation applied for the salt tests until the surface temperature
of the insulation was deemed safe. The exact configuration for both the water and salt tests
has been lost and is a large source of uncertainty that is difficult to quantify. More infor-
mation on how this uncertainty was handled can be found in Chapter 5. The test section
(quartz glass) is uninsulated to maintain optical access for the PIV measurements, which
results in the second largest source of heat loss after the heat exchanger and is heated in the
higher-temperature molten salt cases to counteract these losses.
