3.4
Boundary Condition Verification
The full-loop’s wall is represented by neither a simple fixed-temperature nor fixed-flux bound-
ary condition, but instead uses a custom boundary condition, rcExternalTemperature,
which solves a one-dimensional, cylindrical conduction problem from the pipe’s inner sur-
face through the composite wall layers to find the external temperature that is then used
to solve for external losses from convective and radiative heat transfer. This was done for
a combination of two reasons: the first was that early on, before the work had been scoped
for a thesis-grade problem, the problem was assumed to be simple and that it would not
need to model the full, solid domain and associated conjugate heat transfer. So, to not
scrap the high-quality fluid domain mesh or significantly retrofit the working pipeline, and
since we are only interested in the steady-state solution, a computed thermal circuit could
prove sufficient. The second reason was, with the first established, OpenFOAM V13 did not
have a stock boundary condition that would properly model the heat transfer for the system
of interest as the closest match, externalTemperature, assumed a slab geometry for it’s
thermal circuit model, which under-predicts losses through the boundary due to the greatly
increased surface area resultant from the thick insulation adorning the facility’s exterior.
