Those roundings produce appreciably different values when used, but evaluating the full and
abbreviated equations reveals that Reis et al. did in fact use the full, non-rounded equations
for their analysis. While the scripts that generated the values reported in Reis et al. [9]
could not be recovered, the determination was made by attempting to find a way to calculate
the evaluation temperature used for the reported Prandtl number, Pr that classified each of
the 4 experimental salt cases. The best method found was a simple average of the measured
values of each TP probe (recorded in Celsius), before converting to Kelvin by adding 273
(as opposed to 273.15) for the evaluation. This method reproduced the values of Pr exactly
to reported precision for salt tests 1 and 4, and were 0.01 higher than the published values
for salt test 2 and 3. The rounded versions of the equations published in Reis et al. could
not provide a simultaneous agreement across each of the 4 salt test cases superior to the
full equations using any single TP reading, difference between TP readings, or using TW
measurements in any mixed configuration either, so it was determined with confidence that
the full equations above were used. Attempts at recreating the calculations behind published
results to ensure that the same methodology was being followed within the present work in
the absence of recoverable scripts are present at several points in this work; this one in
particular hints that some of the published calculations were done by hand. The evidence
for this comes up in several places: spreadsheets provided by the experimentalists having
only numeric values without any formulas, the apparent use of +273 as the conversion factor
from Celsius to Kelvin instead of +273.15 is the kind of truncation more likely done by hand,
and the calculated values of Pr for salt test 2 and 3 being off by only 0.01 hints at some
difference in rounding done during the calculation, but lacking direct confirmation, cannot
be confirmed.
