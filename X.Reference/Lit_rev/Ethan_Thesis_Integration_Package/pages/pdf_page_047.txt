4.1
TAMU Natural Circulation Loop
Within the literature of NCL systems, loops with 4 legs are common, and referred to as
"rectangular" on account of these 4-sided systems having a 90-degree rotation between each
leg. The TAMU NCL is special in the sense that has a pair of legs set at an incline rather
than being parallel to the ground in order to encourage a clockwise flow to develop within the
system and avoid some of the instabilities that can result in an inverted flow. This inverted
flow can occur when a rectangular loop is configured with its heater on the bottom leg and a
cooler on its upper leg since the system is symmetric in such a case, the flow can develop in
either a clockwise or counter-clockwise direction [5]. To better differentiate TAMU’s inclined
loop from typical rectangular loops, the facility referenced in this thesis will be referred to
as a "rhombic" loop on account on it’s inclination from parallel and equal-length legs. The
loop operates with two working fluids, water and HITEC molten salt, and uses an identical
pipe configuration for both fluids.
4.1.1
Loop Geometry
The loop’s geometry consists of four legs, each connected at a 90 degree angle with cross
or tee-fittings. Each leg of the loop is nominally 3 feet in length. The top and bottom legs
were bent symmetrically to a nominal incline of 20 degrees with a radius of curvature of
approximately 1 inch, starting roughly 4.325 inches from either end of the pipe. Each pipe
leg had a nominal outer diameter (OD) of 1 inch and a nominal inner diameter (ID) of 0.93
inches and was composed of SS-316 grade stainless steel.
A brief aside is worth mentioning before continuing with the description of the loop’s
geometry; the reported dimensions are mixed between imperial and metrics units, which is
left as reported by the experimentalists instead of all being converted in an effort to avoid
miscommunications and conflicted reports that arose in the conduction of the work within
this thesis.
