4.3.1
HITEC Molten Salt
Reis et al. [9] used thermophysical properties as reported by Idaho National Lab’s (INL)
molten salt database, but a review of the literature for this work found fault in the reported
equations for both the dynamic viscosity and the specific heat capacity of HITEC’s ternary
eutectic mixture. INL itself cites Janz et al. citejanz1981 from 1981 for each of its equations
describing HITEC salt. Janz et al. itself is another repository of equations reported by
other works and cites Kirst et al.
[46] from 1940 for the table of viscosity data it fits
and cites Voskresenskay et al. [47] for the equations used to describe the heat capacity of
the molten salt mixture, who used drop-calorimetry to collect their data. Neither of the
compositions reported in section 148.10 of Janz et al. are for the eutectic ternary mixture
and the reported equation’s uncertainty of 5% still far overshoots modern measurements and
the manufacturer-reported values by nearly 50% for the temperatures studied in this work
(Equation 4.4 produces a value of cp ≈2301 J/kg K vs the modern cp ≈1423.47 J/kg K at
200 ◦C). Drop-calorimetry was used to collect their data for heat capacity.
The equation of state reported for dynamic viscosity is a polynomial fit of an exponential
equation that claims a greater range of validity than the original equation and data the fit
was made to capture from Kirst et al. [46]
