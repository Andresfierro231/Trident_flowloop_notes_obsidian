As mentioned in Section 4.5.1, attempting to close the energy balance across all 8 cases
with this thickness applied to the first order model was unsuccessful, leading to the two
different insulation uncertainties above. The bounds for those insulation thicknesses were
chosen ultimately because of the conflicting information from the reported McMaster entry
(nominally 2" thick of ceramic fiber per layer) and Reis et al. [9] claiming to use 3 layers of
fiberglass insulation; ultimately settling to model the salt cases using the provided McMaster
item and using the difference between the stated and estimated as the thickness bounds. For
water, after establishing that the water cases used a different insulation configuration, the
thinnest variant of the reported insulation was used (a distinctly different part on McMaster-
Carr). The insulation was also sold in thicknesses of 2", 1", and 0.5" under different part
numbers, so the idea for modeling the water’s insulation was an unrecorded purchase of
the thinnest variation. With this though, the first order model and follow-on CFD results
ran too hot, being better served with a modeled thickness of 0.40".
So, barring better
information, the modeled 0.40" configuration was used as the center of the distribution with
the purchasable 0.5" thickness serving as the upper bound, and a symmetric bound being set
at 0.3" as a possible "effective insulation thickness" in the event the insulation was poorly
mounted and caused the system to lose more heat than would otherwise be predicted.
The ambient temperatures use the same ±1.1 K thermocouple uncertainty for the gaus-
sian representing the ambient temperature confidence. There is no data for the ambient
conditions of the experimental facility, so the estimate of the inlet temperature of the heat
exchanger was used. For the external convection coefficient, the Churchill-Chu bounds are
centered between 0.12 and 0.15 for the value of A used in their published fits, where 0.12
was derived for air and 0.15 was used for the general fit. The heater power was reported by
the experimentalists to have a standard uncertainty of 0.93% for powers between 100 and
999 W and an uncertainty of 1% for powers between 1 and 100 W.
