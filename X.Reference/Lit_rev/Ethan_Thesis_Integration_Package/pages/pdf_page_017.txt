Motivation
The global demand for electricity is large and ever-increasing. As Earth’s population contin-
ues to grow, countries develop, and living standards rise; technology advances as well, com-
pounding on the expanding demand for power with electric cars and the explosive growth
of energy-intensive data centers for greater and greater artificial intelligence workloads. To
sustain this growth, it is imperative that we prioritize the capacity of our power infrastruc-
ture [1]. That increased demand cannot be met by simply continuing to expand fossil-fuel
facilities; climate change demands that new capacity, and eventually the existing fossil-fuel
fleet, be filled by low-carbon sources [2].
Nuclear power is one of few technologies that can satisfy both the capacity and climate
demands facing the near future. Nuclear’s ability to supply large amounts of reliable, carbon-
free energy independent of factors that may inhibit other carbon-free technologies such as
weather and geography. Much of the renewed interest in nuclear power is centered on new,
Generation IV reactor concepts. Among theses concepts are Molten Salt Reactors (MSRs),
that promise higher outlet temperature, leading to greater thermal efficiency, while being
