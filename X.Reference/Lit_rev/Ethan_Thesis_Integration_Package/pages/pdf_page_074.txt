5.3
Mesh Generation
5.3.1
Overview
The computational mesh is generated procedurally using classy_blocks [52], a Python
wrapper around OpenFOAM’s blockMesh utility. The mesh is fully structured hexahedral,
avoiding the numerical diffusion and poor convergence rates associated with tetrahedral or
polyhedral meshes in pipe-flow geometries [34].
5.3.2
Cross-Section Topology
Every pipe segment uses either a 12-block or 20-block O-grid cross-section. The standard
12-block topology is composed of:
• 4 core blocks form a central square, avoiding the singular axis point of polar meshes.
• 8 shell blocks form the annular region between the core and the pipe wall.
The 20-block topology is the same as the 12-block topology with an additional 8 blocks
composing a second annular layer when step transitions are required throughout the loop’s
geometry, as when fitting pipes together.
This topological approach produces bounded aspect ratios across the entire cross-section
and ensures smooth boundary layer resolution at the wall. The core-to-pipe radius ratio is
derived from the boundary layer grading parameters to ensure a smooth transition from the
graded boundary layer region to the uniform core.
