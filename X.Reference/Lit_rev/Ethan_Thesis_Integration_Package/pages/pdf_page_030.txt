For any of these modeling choices to be assessed fairly, discretization error must be con-
trolled, and that control begins with the mesh. Truncation error analysis of the finite-volume
method shows that hexahedral cells produce the smallest truncation errors of the common
cell shapes for a given cell size since they have the smallest possible number of face pairs and
thereby have the least amount of truncation for a given cell count in a given volume, with
tetrahedral cells having the largest error [34]. Hernández-Pérez, Abdulkadir, and Azzopardi
[35] demonstrated that hexahedral topologies likewise produce superior volume-of-fluid (VoF)
results compared to tetrahedral or polar meshes for circular pipe cross-sections. Despite be-
ing out of scopre for this work, the applicability to VoF simulations is important because
the meshing infrastructure built for this thesis is intended to outlive it; extending beyond
single-phase flow to two-phase VoF simulation is planned within the digital twin effort at
the University of Texas at Austin (Chapter 1) and both considerations motivated the purely
hexahedral, structured meshes generated for the present work (Chapter 5).
