3.3.2
Mesh Refinement Strategy
With the methodology established and in-line with the first step of determining the GCI
discussed above, we can discuss the mesh refinement strategy used within this work.
The full-loop meshes form a three-level family by uniformly scaling the boundary-layer
and bulk cell size in a hybridized approach to preserve block topology and ensure that a
topologically blind refinement strategy would not muddy refinement where it was important
as in the boundary layer or to overly refine the bulk flow regions since the full-loop mesh
is expensive to compute, especially since pseudo-transient timestepping was needed, which
will be further elaborated on Chapter 5.
For the BC-verification family, a similar approach was used to maintain a consistent
methodology, however, the BC-verification family of meshes were smaller and much faster
to compute overall. This relaxation of computing resources allowed expanding the code-
verification study to a four-level approach using a refinement ratio of r = 2 on a simpler
geometry which permitted better testing of the asymptotic order. For a 3D case, a single
value for a representative cell size, hrep can be found using the equation,
