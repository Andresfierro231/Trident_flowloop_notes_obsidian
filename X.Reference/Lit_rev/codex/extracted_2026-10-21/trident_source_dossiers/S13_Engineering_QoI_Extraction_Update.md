Directly useful engineering map:

- Temperature distribution → assess structural thermal conditions and the heat-removal response; core inlet/outlet temperatures are important, but those locations do not prove global extrema for arbitrary loop geometry.
- Velocity distribution → assess transport and circulation response; a thermal-only TRIDENT model can inform selected flow questions only when hydraulic parameters/observations support them.
- Heat-transfer/source deposition → specify source in fuel fluid versus structures, coolant boundaries and system scenario. External test heating is not identical to spatially varying nuclear energy deposition.
- Composition/phase/gas phenomena → identify transfer limits rather than claim TRIDENT simulates the full reactor safety problem.

Provisional implication: thermal duty, temperature extrema/response and circulation can be motivated by concrete MSR engineering functions. The permissible quantitative claims still depend on our source, geometry, regime and observation similarities. S13 does not supply universal acceptance thresholds or a TRIDENT scaling certificate.
