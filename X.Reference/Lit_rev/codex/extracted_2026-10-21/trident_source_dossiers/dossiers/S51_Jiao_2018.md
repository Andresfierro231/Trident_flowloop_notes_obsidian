## Problem and actual contribution

The study applies modified RMPS-style uncertainty propagation to SINAP's nitrate/nitrite-salt natural-circulation facility, developed with a DRACS prototype in view (§§1–3, PDF pp. 1–3 / 127–129). It adds source-correlation/property uncertainty handling and random selection among heat-transfer correlations in RELAP5-MS, plus regression-based sensitivity ranking. It is prior work against any claim that uncertain parameters, model alternatives, thermal-margin failure events or uncertainty-informed experiment priorities are new to molten-salt circulation.

The failure-probability result is a numerical result conditional on the model, distributions, thresholds and treatment of failed simulations. It is not a measured reactor reliability, nor evidence that every uncertainty was included. The generic RMPS/REPAS history and passive-system classifications are cited prior work, not originals read through S51.

## Geometry, fluids and boundary conditions

§2/Fig. 1 (PDF p. 2 / 128): salt pool 1.7 m high and 1.5 m diameter; circulating NaNO3–NaNO2–KNO3 stated 7–40–53 (basis not specified in that passage). Thirty DHX folding tubes and fifteen NDHX folding tubes, each stated 20/25 mm inner/outer diameter and 3 m length. Heat-exchanger center elevation difference 3 m; adjustable air tower 3–6 m. Three coupled buoyant paths: pool, salt loop and air cooling. Expansion tank included in nodalization.

General heater description is adjustable 20–40 kW; **Table 4 uses 16.5 kW**, salt 543.15 K, air inlet velocity zero, ambient 291.45 K (PDF p. 7 / 133). Do not silently reconcile the power discrepancy. Simulations run 500,000 s toward a relatively steady state (§4.5, PDF p. 6 / 132); this is not a specified reactor decay-power history.

Thresholds (§4.1, PDF p. 4 / 130): high temperature 700.15 K (427°C, decomposition) and low temperature 415.15 K (142°C, melting). These are source-selected salt criteria, not TRIDENT acceptance limits. Observables are maximum/minimum salt temperatures; final-value use in §4.6/Figs. 8–9 needs distinction from a trajectory-wide mission event.

## Equations, closures and uncertainty contract

RELAP5-MS nodalization (§4.3/Fig. 4, PDF pp. 4–5 / 130–131) divides pool, primary loop (19 segments) and air tower. Governing conservation PDEs/numerics/property functions are not fully reproduced. Tube-side Darcy friction \(f=64/Re\), Eq. (1); do not confuse with Fanning \(16/Re\). Eq. (2) uses Nu = 4.36, with the fully developed laminar uniform-flux idealization; laminar flow alone is insufficient to establish every exchanger's applicability.

Table 2 gives three alternative shell-side heat-transfer correlations and their Pr/Re/geometry ranges. They must be checked against actual simulated air-side states before implementation; S51 does not supply all trajectories for that check. Model choice plus a multiplicative uncertainty factor is used for NDHX shell heat transfer (§4.8, PDF p. 8 / 134). Selection probabilities, dependence details and physical calibration are not fully documented; no data-driven posterior model probabilities demonstrated.

Table 3 (PDF p. 6 / 132): 43 parameters, 29 input-deck and 14 source-correlation parameters. Normal and uniform distributions span power, geometry, losses, ambient, properties and heat-transfer multipliers. “Range*” percentages are listed, but the inspected page supplies no resolved definition of the asterisk or whether normal percentages are standard deviations versus bounding intervals. Gaussian truncation/positivity rules are not specified. Do not implement these percentages as sigmas without clarification.

Eq. (7), joint density written as product of marginals, entails an independence assumption. Consistent inlet/outlet air-pressure variations are separately supported (Table 1). Pool and loop properties are separately listed; ensure same-fluid shared-property dependence rather than blindly copying independent factors. “Source correlation parameters” means code correlation coefficients/closures, not proof that joint statistical dependence is modeled.

## Statistical and numerical appraisal

**Reported** §§4.4–4.6, PDF pp. 5–7 / 131–133: 229 samples, a 99% content/95% confidence claim, fifth-order Wilks references, 11 unsuccessful runs (ten crossing melting, one iteration error), 218 completed cases. Six completed high-temperature exceedances plus the iteration-error trajectory are discussed; after the paper's order-statistic treatment, three “effective” high failures and six low failures give reported 0.013 + 0.026 = 0.039.

Do not endorse 0.039 as an independently verified probability. Three issues require separation:

1. **Our audit of the standard iid continuous one-sided tolerance interpretation.** For the maximum of N observations, confidence for content \(\gamma\) is \(1-\gamma^N\). At N=229 and \(\gamma=.99\), this is 0.8999, not .95. For the fifth-largest bound, confidence is \(1-\sum_{k=0}^{4}{N\choose k}(1-\gamma)^k\gamma^{N-k}\), giving 0.08159. Minimum N for .95 is 299 for the maximum, 913 for the fifth-largest. These are our exact-binomial calculations under those assumptions, not numbers reported by S51. The mismatch flags an unresolved interpretation or error, not evidence about the authors' inaccessible code.
2. A tolerance bound on an output quantile and an estimate of event probability are different statistical objects. Removing extremes to define “effective” failure counts does not automatically justify an unbiased event-probability estimator. Eq. (8) defines the ordinary indicator average; its relation to the trimming in the prose is unclear. Raw count arithmetic 17/229 ≈ 0.0742 would require additional assumptions that all ten freezing runs count, the iteration case counts, and modes are disjoint; it is **not** substituted as a corrected reliability result.
3. Eq. (5) writes \(g=A-y\) and assigns failure to \(g\ge0\), appropriate to a low-temperature threshold; overheating requires the opposite inequality or a separately defined margin. A unified two-event implementation is not provided. Preserve sign conventions explicitly.

No grid/time-convergence demonstration or quantitative independent experiment/validation split is provided here. Referenced Cai et al. (2017) validation is not validation read through this paper. Code stopping below melting is not simulated solidification/blockage physics.

## Results worth retaining, with limits

§4.7/Table 5: adjusted regression \(R^2\) 0.846 and 0.852; substantial collinearity led to semi-partial correlation ranking. Table 6 (PDF p. 9 / 135) ranks power, NDHX tube diameter, ambient temperature and shell-side coefficient as influential. Local/linear regression measures over chosen distributions do not establish global ranking under different priors or model families.

§4.8/Fig. 11: shell heat-transfer alternatives substantially change predicted cooling and freezing susceptibility. Authors explicitly lack sufficient experimental data to determine the correlation. Correlation numbering in Table 2 and §4.8 uses different ordering: identify each model by name/formula, not index. No full stochastic validation or calibration from measured thermal responses is shown.

## Transfer, contribution implications and next reading

Direct transfer: declare a mission/QoI, thresholds and uncertainty contract; distinguish code failure from physical threshold events. Conditional: competing cooler closures may dominate margin predictions, motivating discriminating experiments. Geometry, salinity and three coupled loops differ from TRIDENT; do not copy probability distributions or thresholds.

A promising **hypothesis**, not an established gap: observation-consistent inference and targeted model discrimination can produce independently assessed thermal-margin predictions with better uncertainty calibration than freely selected closure multipliers. Need baselines, identifiable estimands, repeated/withheld experiments and probability-calibration evidence. Merely adding Monte Carlo or a Bayesian label is insufficient.

Read official IAEA-TECDOC-1752 methodology/benchmarks next (accessible; no upload needed), alongside available S23 identifiability and S07 design. S30 is a separate challenge to stochastic reduced-loop novelty. Acquire Cai2017 only if this facility's empirical validation is material; exact proceedings identity first needs verification. Do not request it as a fully verified publication yet.
