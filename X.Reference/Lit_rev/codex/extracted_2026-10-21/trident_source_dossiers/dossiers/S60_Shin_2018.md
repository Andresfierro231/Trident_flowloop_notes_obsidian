## Problem, contribution and motivation

The article extends earlier surrogate-fluid work to local flow in a high-Pr oil loop, combining PIV, CFD and boundary-layer interpretation. It motivates passive salt-system heat transfer and refers to earlier Pr/Gr matching (§§1–2). Its own evidence is from **DOWTHERM RP**, not measured molten-salt reactor flow. The earlier similarity derivation and heat-transfer correlation are cited prior work, not newly established by this experiment.

Its strong prior-art relevance is that nonuniform local velocities, near-wall vortical behavior and a helical-flow interpretation are already studied experimentally/numerically. A claim that local recirculation is simply absent from the field is untenable.

## Equations, conventions and reproducibility

Eqs1–4 (PDF3 / 1352): Pr=mu cp/k,Gr=g beta(Tw−Tb)D³/nu²,Ra=GrPr,Re=rho VD/mu. Use **mass-specific** cp in Pr; Table1 instead lists **volumetric** capacity. Table5 cp is kJ/(kg K), nu in10^-6m²/s, k W/(m K). Unit normalization is essential.

Table5 (PDF6 / 1355) lists properties at423/473/623K and Pr23/16/9; those are representative hotter-fluid values, not the actual Pr at all lower-temperature simulation states. Figure19 shows much larger actual Pr values for the power sweep. Source manufacturer fitting equations/coefficients are not fully reproduced; consult the exact product sheet before coding.

Eqs5–6 (PDF6) give boundary-layer thickness approximations, including delta/delta_t approximately Pr^(1/3), with the text stating.6≤Pr≤50. Table8 (PDF13) includes Pr^(1/3)=5.13 at200W, implying Pr≈135 by our arithmetic; several cases exceed the stated range. Do not use the comparison as a uniformly in-range theoretical validation.

Eq7 prints \((T-T_s)/(T_s-T_b)=.99\). For an ordinary monotone profile between hotter surface Ts and cooler bulk Tb, the left-hand side is nonpositive, so the stated positive criterion is inconsistent with that convention. A conventional normalized-distance criterion could differ in sign/definition; **do not silently replace the reported equation**.

§4.3 uses the average velocity along the **loop centerline** as a free-stream reference and99% as the boundary-layer criterion. This is not necessarily the cross-section bulk velocity, particularly with central minima/near-wall maxima. Multiple crossings or nonmonotonic profiles can make a99% thickness nonunique.

No full governing/numerical CFX specification, time-step refinement, iteration criterion, property fit coefficients or planar measurement operator is supplied. Laminar CFD unsteadiness/vorticity does not by itself establish turbulent flow.

## Results, uncertainty and skeptical appraisal

Mesh study: five node counts517774→2246420 (Table4/Fig7, PDF5). Bulk temperature56.28→61.42°C; velocity rounded.018/.021m/s. Authors report convergence within.4% near2million nodes (§3.3, PDF9). No formal GCI/order estimate, temporal convergence or uncertainty band for local extrema/vortex structure. Rounded bulk velocity is insufficient to bound local-field error.

PIV average/instantaneous patterns (Figs12–13, PDF7–8; §4.1, PDF10–11) show zigzag motion and local vortices in the observed plane. A3D helical rising structure is proposed from these observations and CFD (Figs14/17–18). It is a plausible combined interpretation, **not independently measured full-volume helix extent**.

Table6 (PDF7) compares a broad experimental velocity range.02–.07m/s with five CFD values including a central.009m/s and near-wall.069m/s. This is a limited comparison; no pointwise residual/uncertainty covariance or independent thermal-field validation accompanies the claim of CFD validation.

Power, mean temperature, properties, Pr, velocity, buoyancy and thermal driving vary together (§4/Table7/Figs19–23). The observed trend is not an intervention varying only Pr at fixed other similarity groups. An isolated causal “Pr effect” or universal onset threshold is not established.

The paper interprets high Pr as favoring convection, but Pr is a diffusivity ratio; advective-versus-conductive transport additionally depends on velocity/length, e.g. Pe=RePr. Near-wall temperature gradients are related to heat flux only with known conductivity and an appropriate resolved gradient. The study does not isolate an experimental incremental heat-transfer coefficient caused by the vortical structure.
