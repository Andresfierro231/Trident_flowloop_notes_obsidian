## Problem, contribution and evidence

The paper addresses models whose states are only partly observed. A fit may reproduce measured outputs while parameters and hidden states remain ambiguous. It distinguishes structural redundancy from practical non-identifiability at a specified likelihood threshold, profiles parameters by reoptimizing the rest, and uses resulting trajectory variation to suggest measurements or reductions.

Its application is a JAK–STAT biological pathway calibrated to an existing experimental dataset. New proposed measurements are **hypothetical data used to reassess identifiability**, not a new completed experiment. The method transfers to thermal systems as inference methodology, not as evidence about thermal mechanisms.

## Observation, parameters and objective

Eqs1–3,PDF2/1924 distinguish dynamical states x, inputs u, parameters p, observation map g with scale/offset parameters s, and initial state x(0). The unknown vector includes p,x(0),s. This is directly relevant to hidden insulation temperature, sensor bias/scale and uncertain initial fluid/wall splits.

The reported least-squares criterion is
\[
\chi^2(\theta)=\sum_{k,l}\left[\frac{y^D_{kl}-y_k(\theta,t_l)}
{\sigma^D_{kl}}\right]^2,
\]
Eq4,PDF2/1924. It equals a constant minus2log likelihood under the stated Gaussian error model and the corresponding variance assumptions, Eq6. It is dimensionless. For TRIDENT, shared bias, autocorrelation, uncertain inputs or parameter-dependent covariance require a different joint likelihood; the diagonal weighted sum is not automatically appropriate.

Reported profile, Eq10,PDF4/1926, visually checked:
\[
\chi^2_{\rm PL}(\theta_i)=\min_{\theta_{j\ne i}}\chi^2(\theta).
\]
The other parameters are reoptimized. A curve obtained while holding them fixed is a slice, not this profile. Profiling is optimization, not marginalization over a Bayesian posterior.

The article works in log parameters for positive quantities. Signed biases, zero-capacity limiting models and signed circulation require separate parameterization; not every unknown can be logged.

## What the profiles establish and do not establish

Structural redundancy produces flat directions with unchanged observables (Eq9,§2.3,PDF3/1925). Practical non-identifiability has a distinct optimum but a profile that stays below the confidence threshold toward at least one limit. Both-sided unbounded confidence regions are not alone proof of structural redundancy: flat invariance versus a shallow, nonflat profile matters.

The application identifies coupled combinations of a rate, initial concentration and observation scales, Eq11/Fig4,PDF5/1927. It explicitly warns that recovering functional relations uniquely from a one-parameter profile requires a one-dimensional manifold; higher-dimensional ambiguities require further analysis.

Table1,PDF6/1928 shows the practically unidentified log parameter p3 has profile interval[−1.14,+∞), whereas its Hessian interval is[−1.14,+.15]. These are biological log-parameter coordinates, not thermal values. The contrast illustrates how local curvature can miss a long nonlinear valley.

The paper calls likelihood intervals “finite sample,” but a χ² cutoff is not automatically exact in finite, correlated, bounded or nonregular problems. Its coverage and degrees of freedom must be justified for the actual likelihood. Our implementation should test finite-sample calibration if the inferential claim requires it.

## Experiments, reduction and appraisal

Section4,PDF4/1926 propagates parameter sets along profiles to find where model trajectories differ, proposing observations/times that might distinguish them. Section5,PDF6/1928 tests added absolute-scale and species-ratio information using hypothetical measurements. The qualitative analogue is an independent energy/inventory anchor or an observation that separates two thermal states—not simply more readings of the same ambiguous output.

Section4 also links a small-parameter limit to reduced-model comparison. Failure to reject a reduced model on current data does not establish predictive equivalence at new conditions. Boundary limits can violate ordinary likelihood-ratio regularity.

A finite numerical scan cannot prove a global flat symmetry or global identifiability. Parameter bounds, optimizer failure and local minima can shape profiles; Fig3's caption explicitly notes remote minima. Use exact structural arguments where possible, multiple optimization starts and clear range/sensitivity reporting.

The paper's broad “only remedy” language for structural ambiguity concerns its fixed model/observation setting. In TRIDENT, independent parameter knowledge, admissible input changes or reparameterization can also alter what is identifiable. Likewise, nonunique parameters do not imply that every prediction is uncertain: some observable combinations can be well constrained.
